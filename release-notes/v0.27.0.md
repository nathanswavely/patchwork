**Read the first section before you upgrade an instance that has been running
for more than two months.** Everything else here is ordinary good news.

## A bug that rotates your admins, over and over

A patch whose admins go quiet loses their seats to the inactivity sweep — that
is by design (docs/adr/051). What was not by design: the member promoted to
fill the gap was judged on a joining date *already* older than the vacate
threshold, so they were inactive the instant they were appointed, vacated on
the next pass, and the role walked through the entire membership for ever,
ringing a bell at every step. A five-patch test world left alone for 320
simulated days produced **1,348 succession events and 1,347 seat vacations**,
and ended with every founder a plain member of the patch they created.

It does not need an unusual patch to happen. Activity counts votes, proposals
and proposal comments; **posting events is deliberately not participation** —
a seat on the council goes quiet, not an account. So a venue that runs events
every week and holds no votes reads as entirely inactive, and the Formal
template vacates after sixty days of that. Migration 041 backfilled
`inactivity_days: 90` onto nearly every pre-existing patch.

**Before you upgrade, find out whether it has already started:**

```sh
sqlite3 /path/to/patchwork.db \
  "SELECT COUNT(*) FROM audit_log WHERE action IN ('membership.succession','membership.seat_vacated');"
```

Zero means you are clear; upgrade and it can never start. Anything else means
some of your patches have had their admins rotated, and **this release does not
put them back** — it stops the rotation and leaves the record of what happened.
You will want to restore those admins by hand afterwards, from the audit log,
which names every promotion and every vacated seat in order. If you need to
stop the bleeding before you can upgrade, setting `inactivity_days` to 0 on the
affected patches disables the sweep for them and changes nothing else.

The fix: absence is measured from the later of the person's last governance
act, when they joined, and **when they took the role** (`memberships.
role_since`, migration 072). Interim promotion now requires presence rather
than tenure alone, so every interim admin is somebody the sweep would not
vacate — the loop terminates by construction rather than by a counter. Vacating
an admin empties their chair, and refilling follows the patch's leadership
model instead of promoting by tenure regardless. See docs/adr/102.

## Elections run end to end

They did not before. A patch born `elected` never held an election; a contest
that settled nothing left no seat behind, so nothing was ever due again and the
calendar fell silent; the one button on a contest page stood *you* whatever you
had come to do; and nothing anywhere took a name back off a slate.

- **Seats are furniture you can count and arrange.** A patch admin adds a
  vacant chair or removes an empty one, and sets a seat's term end — the box
  that makes staggering real. Filling a chair is still nomination and
  ratification, so arranging the furniture grants nobody anything
  (docs/adr/100).
- **A contest names its chairs.** It records which seats it is for, so a
  staggered council can put up one chair without unseating the colleagues
  nobody voted about (docs/adr/103).
- **You can put another member forward, and take your own name back.** The
  bylaws always said you could; now the product does, with
  `DELETE /api/v1/proposals/{id}/candidates/me` while nominations are open
  (docs/adr/107).
- **A contest nobody stood in settles when nominations close** rather than
  opening a ballot with no names on it and running a fortnight to reach the
  answer already true (docs/adr/106).
- **A contest the calendar opened is signed by the calendar**, not by whichever
  admin had been there longest — whose bell its comments were ringing
  (docs/adr/109).
- **Saving a ballot says so**, the nomination window keeps its date after it
  closes, and a result tells the patch rather than only the winner.

## A vote that misses quorum stops hanging open

Under quorum at close, a proposal used to stay open for ever while refusing
ballots. It now **lapses**: nobody decided it, and every surface says so in
those words rather than calling it rejected (docs/adr/097).

Alongside it: a proposal now reaches people who join after it opens, a turnout
notice at the halfway mark tells the people who still owe a ballot how many
are still needed, and a vote that carries tells somebody — including whether
it is in effect or waiting on an admin.

## Invite-only patches have a door

There was none. `JoinNode` refused, instance invite links create accounts
rather than admit anyone to a patch, and there was no add-member route — so a
person told "you're in" could only Follow. An admin now invites by username and
the person accepts or declines; until they answer they are not a member of
anything (docs/adr/098, migration 071).

## Smaller things worth knowing

- **Changing a timezone asks first.** A patch's zone, or the whole quilt's,
  now refuses a change that would alter what any event *reads* as until
  somebody says whether to keep the clock times or leave the instants where
  they are. It counts what it would touch first — and a patch that named its
  own zone is never moved by the quilt's (docs/adr/101, docs/adr/105). A
  timezone must also name a place: `EST` is refused, because it is an hour
  wrong from March to November.
- **A patch can be public without publishing who is in it**
  (`public_member_list`, migration 069, docs/adr/095).
- **A patch younger than its own voting-tenure bar has no bar**, so a new
  co-op can decide things in its first month instead of lapsing its own first
  vote with an electorate of nobody (docs/adr/098).
- **The rules editor says what your numbers cost**: "4 of the 8 people who can
  vote today must cast a ballot", what consensus does before you choose it, and
  how many of today's members clear the tenure bar (docs/adr/104).
- **A recovery code can pass the step-up gate**, so somebody whose device
  cannot make a passkey is not locked out of promoting an admin, deleting
  their account, or anything else behind it (docs/adr/099).
- **"Repeats weekly" is gone.** It was stored and displayed and expanded
  nowhere — not into occurrences, not into the ICS feed. Rows that carry the
  word render it as the organizer's claim, with the caveat that only this date
  is on the calendar (docs/adr/101).

## Upgrading

`docker compose pull && docker compose up -d`, after the check in the first
section.

Five migrations run (069 to 073). Four add a column. **071 rebuilds the
`memberships` table** to widen a CHECK constraint, carrying every column and
all three indexes; no existing row changes value, since nothing could write the
new status before this release. Take your usual backup first — the same advice
as every release, and this is the one where you will be glad of it.

Rolling back to v0.26.1 works: the previous image reads this database, ignores
the columns it does not know about, and no migration rewrites data it would
miss. What it does not do is put back admins that were already rotated before
you upgraded.

This release is marked **breaking** for one reason only: on an instance where
the succession bug has already run, upgrading is not the whole job, and an
unattended updater should stop and let a person read the first section.
